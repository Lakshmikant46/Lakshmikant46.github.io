Laxmikanta Sarangi

Ph.D. Scholar in Economics, IIT Roorkee
Statistical Officer, Directorate of Economics & Statistics, Government of Odisha

Firm-level market power, monopsony, and the (mis)allocation of labour in Indian manufacturing.

I am a fifth-year Ph.D. scholar in Economics at IIT Roorkee, where my work sits at the juncture of macroeconomic development, productivity, and firm and labour-market dynamics. My primary research examines the dynamics of firms' labour-market power within and across industries, and I am equally drawn to the (mis)allocation of labour in the Indian manufacturing sector. Alongside my doctoral work, I serve as a Statistical Officer at the Directorate of Economics & Statistics, Government of Odisha. I am keen to collaborate on research related to my thesis topic.

The geography of market power in Indian manufacturing

Explore the interactive map →

Five switchable layers — industrial concentration, markups, wage markdowns, monopsony incidence, and misallocation — for every Indian state, estimated from 423,127 plant-year observations of the Annual Survey of Industries, 2012–13 to 2022–23. From my working paper Protection without Power.

Research interests

  • Market power: markups, wage markdowns, and monopsony in the production-function tradition
  • Labour (mis)allocation and productivity in Indian organised manufacturing
  • Trade, exchange rates, and the macro determinants of market power
  • Spatial economics: agglomeration, rural–urban labour markets
  • Empirical industrial organisation and applied production theory
  • Industrial statistics: the ASI and the IIP
